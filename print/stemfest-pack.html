Two teams. Two bots each. Two minutes.

Your group splits into Red and Blue. Two of each team drive the bots; the rest are the brains on the iPad. Your score is STEM points + bot points, so the brains and the drivers both win it for you.

What you'll learn

⚡ Forces & levers: how a flipper lifts a robot💻 Code & logic: loops, binary, IF statements
🕳️ Circuits & electricity: volts, amps, Ohm's law🤖 Sensors & robot brains: how robots sense the world
⚙️ Gears & motion: speed, RPM, gear ratios🌪️ Boss challenge: energy, momentum and more

Got one wrong? The iPad explains the right answer, so you learn something either way.

How does the arena work?

Each iPad talks over WiFi to a small server. The server sends the command to a Raspberry Pi, a credit-card-sized computer, which switches the arena's motors, solenoids and servos. The flipper is a solenoid: a coil that becomes an electromagnet and kicks it up. Look through the clear front of the arena base to see the tech!

⚠ Safety: the arena hazards only move when our referee has armed it, and they switch themselves off after a few seconds. Never reach into the arena. Bots go in and out only when the referee says so.

For teachers: what your group just did

Your class played Earn to Fire with InfoSec Battle Bots at STEMfest Newcastle. In two teams, students answered STEM questions to fire real hazards in a robot combat arena while classmates drove the robots. Every wrong answer came with an explanation.

Topics covered (KS3)

Forces & leversFulcrums, Newton's laws, weight = mass × g, centre of gravity
Circuits & electricityOhm's law (V = I × R), P = V × I, series circuits, fuses, solenoids
Gears & motionSpeed = distance / time, RPM, gear ratios, circumference
Code & logicVariables, loops, IF statements, binary, AND / NOT
Sensors & robot brainsUltrasonic ranging, inputs vs outputs, actuators, microcontrollers

Back in class: five follow-up questions

  1. A spinner weapon spins twice as fast. Why does it hit four times harder? (KE = ½mv²)
  2. A 12 V battery drives a 4 Ω motor. What current flows, and how much power does it use?
  3. Design a pit-avoiding robot: which sensor would you use, and what would your IF statement say?
  4. Why do battle robots use a low centre of gravity? Sketch a robot that is hard to flip.
  5. Count to 31 in binary on one hand. How high can you count with two hands?

Answers: (1) energy grows with speed squared, so 2² = 4; (2) 3 A, 36 W; (3) e.g. a downward light or ultrasonic sensor: IF floor not detected THEN reverse; (4) it must be lifted further before it tips; (5) 1023 (10 bits).

How the arena works

The iPads talk over WiFi to a Windows server running a Node.js program. It checks each answer, then sends commands to a Raspberry Pi, which switches the arena's motors, solenoids and servos. It is real networking, coding and electronics: a good talking point for careers in engineering and cyber security.
